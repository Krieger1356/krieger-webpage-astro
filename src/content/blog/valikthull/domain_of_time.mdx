## Sioraíocht Information
![Cathair nan Lasair](/images/doti.webp)

Time does not flow here—it eddies, pools, and occasionally backwashes. The Domain is less a geography than a condition, a state of perpetual becoming and un-becoming. Past, present, and future exist in a churning, luminous superposition, like light refracted through a shattered prism. Landscapes are not fixed; they are suggestions. A traveler might step into a sun-drenched meadow only to watch it frost over in the space of a breath, then crumble to dust, then sprout anew. Rivers flow upstream and down simultaneously, their waters a braid of what-was and what-will-be. The sky is a tapestry of conflicting hours: dawn bleeding into dusk, noon sharing the same patch of ether with a starless midnight. To walk here is to walk through memory and prophecy at once, and only a will anchored fiercely in the “now” can navigate without being unraveled into echoes.

At the heart of this chaos lies the Garden of Still Time. It is not a garden of plants, but of moments—a collection of instants plucked from the flux and asked to remain. Here, time has consented to pause. Every petal is suspended at the peak of its bloom, every dewdrop hangs in mid-fall, a perfect, glittering sphere of arrested motion. The air is motionless, scentless, and silent; sound does not travel, it simply is, then is not. The gravel paths make no noise underfoot. The light has no source and casts no shadow. It is a bubble of absolute stasis within the temporal maelstrom, a sanctuary built not by force, but by request. It is the only place in all of Valik’thull where “now” is not a fleeting point between “then” and “next,” but a permanent, breathless residence. To enter the garden is to step outside the stream of causality, into a quiet so profound it feels like the universe holding its breath.

This garden is home to Osiris An-Drasta, the Prince of Time. Her hair is long and white, her eyes perpetually covered by a fine black silk blindfold—a choice of sensory deprivation to better hear time’s discordant song. She wears robes of black and gold, regal yet simple, her bare feet planted on the timeless gravel of her path. She moves with a preternatural calm, for she is not fighting the flux; she is its curator. Her power is not to control time, but to ask things of it—and often, it listens.

### Flow of Time

<Details summary="Flow of Time">
Osiris is the original, the only Prince who has never died, never passed her fragment to another. Her mind is both ancient and newborn. She suffers from a cyclical amnesia, her long-term memory scrubbed clean each time a new generation of other Princes rises. She remembers her name, her power, her garden, but the context of her long life—the treaties, the wars, the faces of other Princes—dissolves like mist. This amnesia is not an accident; it is the desperate defense mechanism of the terrified god-fragment within her, a shard of a dead deity that fears nothing more than being reunited with its other pieces. To forget is to remain safe, isolated, and alone.

Thus, the Garden of Still Time is also her memory palace. Every frozen blossom, every unmoving statue, is a clue she left for herself before the last forgetting. She wanders its paths, piecing together the story of eternity from the fragments she preserved in stillness. She is the oldest being in existence, yet she is always meeting the world for the first time. Her tragedy is her power; her power is her cage. And in the silent heart of her garden, she waits, but for what, she can never quite recall.
</Details>
